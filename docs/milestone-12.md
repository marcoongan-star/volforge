# Milestone 12 — Paired-path agent distributions

## Product slice

VolForge now evaluates the market-making and directional policies on hundreds of identical seeded earnings paths. The comparison reports mean P&L, standard deviation, probability of profit, the 5th percentile, and 5% expected shortfall for each agent.

The public example uses 500 paired synthetic paths with base seed 14000. Its displayed values are generated by the tested experiment code, not invented dashboard metrics.

## Why paths are paired

If each strategy saw different random markets, part of their difference would be sampling noise. Trial `i` gives both agents the path from `base_seed + i`, so the paired difference isolates how their positions react to the same terminal market.

```text
seeded stock path ──┬─> delta-hedged maker fill ─> maker P&L
                    |
                    └─> directional position ─────> directional P&L
                                      |
                         pair the two trial results
```

## Simplified strategies

- The maker alternates whether the synthetic customer buys at the ask or sells at the bid, holds one option position, and establishes an initial delta hedge.
- The directional agent chooses buy, sell, or hold once from its forecast rule and carries that option position to terminal value.
- Both pay the same stated transaction-cost input.

This is a controlled experiment, not a claim that either policy represents a production strategy.

## Downside metrics

The 5th percentile is a loss threshold: 95% of simulated results are at or above it. Expected shortfall averages the worst 5% of results, so it describes tail severity after that threshold is crossed. Reporting both prevents a positive mean from hiding a dangerous left tail.

## Failure modes and boundaries

- A favorable forecast is an input, not evidence that the model predicts real earnings.
- One initial delta hedge leaves gamma and jump risk.
- Alternating flow direction removes order-flow prediction from this experiment.
- Results change with volatility, costs, confidence, forecast, seed range, and position limits.
- Comparing objectives does not prove that the higher sample mean is the better real strategy.

## Interview explanation

“I used common random numbers: both agents face the same seeded path in each trial. That lowers noise in the strategy difference. I report mean and profit frequency, but also the 5th percentile and expected shortfall because a positive average can coexist with severe tail risk. The experiment is replayable and explicitly synthetic.”
